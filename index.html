Baseline example. At the Moderate tail, a $250k layer attaching at $100k has an expected layer loss of $29,650 per claim. The $350k limit gives an ILF of 1.48× because expected loss capped at $350k is 1.48 times expected loss capped at the $100k attachment.

Model assumptions and formulas
  • Severity. The synthetic lognormal model holds the mean claim size at $100k across tails. Its log-scale location is μ = ln(100k) − σ²/2. This illustration excludes client data, allocated loss adjustment expenses, and treaty terms.
  • Limited expected loss. For a limit x, L(x) = E[X]Φ(z−σ) + xΦ(−z), where z = (ln(x) − μ)/σ. The standard normal CDF uses the Abramowitz and Stegun 26.2.17 approximation.
  • Layer and ILF. For attachment a and coverage limit b, the layer width is b − a, expected excess loss is L(b) − L(a), and the ILF is L(b)/L(a). The selected attachment is the ILF base.
  • Breakdown. P(Exceed) is the probability a claim exceeds the attachment for the excess row and the coverage limit for the above-limit row. Layer burn is expected excess loss divided by layer width. Average attached loss is the expected layer payment conditional on the claim exceeding attachment.
  • Simulation check. The Rust/WebAssembly model runs 65,536 fixed-seed (2026) lognormal draws to cross-check the analytical layer loss. The reported delta is finite-sample Monte Carlo sampling error relative to the exact closed-form value (shrinking as 1/√N), which naturally widens at high attachments and heavier tails.

Baseline example. On Mack’s (1993) RAA 10×10 reinsurance triangle at a 1.00× tail, volume-weighted Chain Ladder estimates $52.1M of unpaid IBNR on $161.0M reported ($213.1M ultimate), with a distribution-free Mack standard error of $26.9M (51.6% CV).

Reserving methods and benchmark datasets
  • Chain Ladder & Mack (1993). Volume-weighted age-to-age factors project each origin’s latest diagonal to ultimate; Mack’s distribution-free variance formula decomposes process and parameter risk per accident year and across the aggregate portfolio.
  • Bornhuetter–Ferguson & Cape Cod. BF weights expected a priori ultimate by the unreported fraction 1 − 1/CDF; Cape Cod estimates the expected loss ratio directly from used-up exposure (earned premium divided by CDF).
  • Benchmark triangles. RAA (Mack 1993, Automatic Facultative GL, in $000s), GenIns (Taylor & Ashe 1983 / England & Verrall 1999, paid), and US Industry Auto (Friedland 2010, paid & earned premium, in $000s). For RAA and GenIns, illustrative exposure vectors are scaled to mature volume for BF/Cape Cod comparisons.
  • Client-side Rust/WASM. Computed directly in your browser by the zero-dependency reserving_kernels.wasm C-ABI build from reserving-kernels.
